# Population & Demographics — Saigon SEZ (2226)

**Baseline:** Real Ho Chi Minh City, 2026 — ~10.5 million city proper, ~93.5% Kinh / ~5.78% Hoa, growing ~2.3%/year 
**2226 estimate:** Comparable to or higher than today's city-proper population, compressed into a far smaller defended footprint (see [[Saigon SEZ - City Geography]]) — density well beyond any present-day megacity district 
**Character:** A genuine melting pot, unusually cosmopolitan even by SEZ standards

## Ethnic & Cultural Breakdown

|Group|Share|Notes|
|---|---|---|
|Vietnamese (Kinh)|65%|Still the majority, but down sharply from today's ~93.5% — diluted by 200 years of multi-directional refugee inflow, not displaced by any single group.|
|Chinese (Hoa / Cholon)|15%|Up dramatically from today's ~5.78%. Internally layered: an old, deeply assimilated lineage (centuries deep, culturally closer to [[Cholon]]'s Minh Hương-style history) alongside the newer, more linguistically and culturally mainland-tied second wave — the layer most likely to draw suspicion. See [[Cholon]].|
|South Asian archipelago diaspora (Malay, Indonesian, and related)|10%|The Nusantara-descended population, tied to [[Nusantara Diaspora Resettlement and Workforce Solutions]]'s presence and the wider archipelago collapse. Concentrated near the company's garrisons and labor housing.|
|Khmer and Cham|5%|Displaced from a drowned and dam-throttled Mekong Delta and wider mainland Southeast Asia, per the regional picture established in the GDD.|
|Japanese / Korean / European / American|5%|Larger than a purely administrative elite would require — see Research Frontier below. Not confined to the upper platform levels the way [[The Compact]]'s leadership is, though that's still where the wealthiest of this group live.|

## Why the Foreign Population Runs Deeper Than Just Leadership

Saigon SEZ isn't only a resource and manufacturing hub — it's become a **regulatory frontier for technological and biological research**, effectively a wild west for work that wouldn't be permitted back home. Loose oversight, a corpo-gov more interested in outcomes than ethics review, and deep pools of desperate, undocumented labor willing to participate in exchange for money or medical access have made the zone a magnet for researchers, technicians, and biohackers — not just executives. This is a major reason the Japanese/Korean/European/American population is a full 5% rather than a rounding error confined to boardrooms: a real research economy, legitimate and gray-market both, draws its own population independent of corporate administration. [[Aveline Biogenetics]] is the most prominent legitimate face of this, but far from the only one.

## Texture Notes

- Street-level language is genuinely layered: Vietnamese as the lingua franca, Cantonese/Teochew/Hokkien and Mandarin audible throughout Cholon, a Malay/Indonesian creole around Nusantara-linked neighborhoods, Khmer in pockets, and Japanese/Korean/English in and around Compact-controlled spaces and research facilities.
- The "melting pot" framing is deliberate and should never read as romanticized — this diversity exists because of mass displacement, corporate exploitation, and a regulatory vacuum, not because Saigon SEZ is some enlightened cosmopolitan project. See below.
- Character creation and NPC writing should feel free to draw from any of these threads without every character needing to explain their background — by 2226 this mix is simply what Saigon looks like.

## Racial and Ethnic Tension — Worse, Not Better

Proximity did not produce harmony. As global instability deepened over the two centuries before the story's present, nations turned inward — protectionist, insular, prioritizing their own survival over cooperation (the same trajectory of democratic backsliding and resource nationalism documented in the setting's founding research). That instinct didn't stay confined to national borders. It came into Saigon SEZ with everyone who arrived, and forced proximity between groups with no particular reason to trust one another did not dissolve it — if anything, competition for scarce housing, safe platform levels, and SEZAC's limited goodwill sharpened it.

Concretely, this means:

- **[[Cholon]]'s "suspected fifth column" status is not an isolated case** — it's the sharpest, most geopolitically loaded example of a pattern that runs through the whole zone. Every group here carries some version of being watched, blamed, or scapegoated by someone else.
- **Old-world resentments didn't get left behind.** Whatever tensions, hierarchies, or prejudices a given community carried before displacement mostly traveled with them, and picked up new grievances (over jobs, platform-level housing, corpo favoritism) on arrival.
- **SEZAC and [[The Compact]] have no strong incentive to fix this**, and some incentive to let it simmer — a population divided against itself is less able to organize against the corpo-gov running it.
- **This should read as texture, not spectacle.** Everyday friction, casual prejudice, uneven policing, and NPCs with real (not cartoonish) grudges — not city-wide race riots as a default state. The tension is structural and constant, not a single dramatic event.
