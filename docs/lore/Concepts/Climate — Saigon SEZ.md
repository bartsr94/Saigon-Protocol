# Climate — Saigon SEZ (2226)

**Region:** Saigon Special Economic Zone, southern Vietnam 
**Baseline:** Extrapolated from real-world 2026 climate data and published warming projections, carried forward ~200 years under this setting's "hothouse Earth" premise 
**Key hazard:** Routine wet-bulb conditions exceeding unmodified human survivability during wet-season afternoons 
**Defining infrastructure response:** Engineered climate control is life support, not comfort — see [[Nusantara Diaspora Resettlement and Workforce Solutions]] and the wider drowned-Delta context in the GDD regional picture

## Saigon 2026

Real Ho Chi Minh City sits in a tropical monsoon climate: highs of 32-37°C nearly year-round, humidity of roughly 82-83% in the June-September wet season dropping to 61-67% in the January-March dry stretch, about two meters of rain a year concentrated May-to-November, and a typhoon season running October-December. This is the climate Saigon Protocol's 2226 extrapolates forward from.

## The Documented Trajectory

Real climate science already projects severe change well before this setting's 200-year mark:

- Southeast Asian cities are projected to see roughly a **4°C hot-season temperature increase by 2050** versus 2025 levels.
- By 2080, up to **1.1 billion people across South and Southeast Asia could face 30+ days of extreme heat annually.**
- Vietnam's share of the year classified as "warm days" could rise from ~25% at 2°C of global warming to **as much as 57.5% at 4°C**, with the Mekong Delta and southern regions — Saigon's region — facing the country's most severe heat impacts.
- "Feels-like" danger already regularly exceeds the thermometer: during 2025's regional heatwave, Bangkok's heat index reached conditions that _felt like_ 52°C at a lower actual air temperature.
- The commonly cited "35°C wet-bulb = death" survivability threshold is now understood to be conservative. Physiology-based research puts real limits closer to 25.8–34.1°C wet-bulb for young adults (lower for the elderly), with intolerable heat stress documented as low as 25°C wet-bulb in hot, dry air and ~31°C in humid conditions. Given Saigon's wet season already runs 80%+ humidity at high temperature _today_, this margin is uncomfortably thin even now.

## Saigon, 2226

- **Average annual temperature: ~34–36°C**, up from today's ~28°C. What today counts as an extreme heatwave day is an ordinary Tuesday.
- **Wet-bulb conditions routinely cross unmodified survivability thresholds during wet-season afternoons.** Not a rare disaster — a standing, expected hazard. This is the direct, mechanical justification for why heat-tolerance genetic modification became normalized rather than optional (see [[Aveline Biogenetics]]), and why the SEZ's interior climate control is existential infrastructure rather than a comfort feature.
- **No meaningful night time cooling.** Overnight lows no longer drop far below today's daytime highs. This removes the recovery window heat-stressed bodies need, and is arguably more dangerous than the daytime peak — exterior night work remains genuinely hazardous, not just daytime labor.
- **Seasons intensify rather than blur.** The underlying monsoon circulation persists (this isn't a uniform, featureless hot climate), but each season sharpens toward its own extreme:
    - **Dry season** — longer, harsher, closer to genuine drought. Unprotected exposure is dangerous even for modified people doing hard labor.
    - **Wet season** — shorter but far more violent. Regional precipitation modelling suggests flat-to-declining total rainfall alongside sharply increasing storm intensity (tropical cyclone rainfall rates are projected to rise roughly 14% globally per 2°C of warming) — meaning fewer rain days, but catastrophic, concentrated deluges rather than a gentle, evenly-spread monsoon.
- **Typhoons: fewer, but far worse.** The global pattern holds — total cyclone counts flat or declining, but the proportion reaching the most severe categories rises substantially, bringing worse wind, storm surge, and rainfall. For a city already fighting subsidence and sea-level rise, a 2220s typhoon is a full stress test of every sea wall and pump station the SEZ has — a natural high-stakes seasonal event (evacuation pressure, infrastructure failure, a case that needs solving before landfall).
- **The sky is not clean.** Industrial haze from a corpo-gov with limited environmental incentive, particulate drift from failing ecosystems elsewhere, and heat/exhaust vented by the city's own climate infrastructure combine into a hazy, filtered, artificially-lit default sky — the noir aesthetic has a climatological cause, not just a stylistic one.

## Narrative & Design Hooks

- Interior vs. exterior isn't just a location toggle — it's a survivability toggle. Scenes set outdoors during a wet-season afternoon can carry real stakes (Vitality drain, per the GDD wellbeing system) independent of any human antagonist.
- A major typhoon making landfall is a strong candidate for a set-piece story beat: infrastructure strain, evacuation politics (who gets moved to safety and who doesn't touches directly on the setting's class themes), and a ticking clock.
- The lack of nighttime relief undercuts a classic noir device (the cool night as a break from the day's heat) — worth deciding deliberately whether Saigon Protocol keeps that convention or subverts it, since it doesn't hold up under this climate model.
