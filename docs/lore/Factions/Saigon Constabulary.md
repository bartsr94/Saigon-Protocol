# Saigon Constabulary

**Type:** Police force, formally a department of [[SEZAC]] 
**Detective's division:** Criminal Investigation Division (CID) 
**Legitimacy:** Real — genuine jurisdiction, real badges, real casework 
**Funding:** Chronically strained; quietly supplemented by [[The Compact]] when a case matters to them 
**Rival/friction:** [[Nusantara Diaspora Resettlement and Workforce Solutions]] over contested jurisdiction

## Overview

The Saigon Constabulary is not a fig leaf the way some of SEZAC's other functions are. Most of what it does is real, unglamorous police work — robbery, assault, missing persons, the ordinary crime of a dense, stratified city — carried out by cops who are, for the most part, exactly what they appear to be. The name deliberately echoes the real-world colonial constabulary tradition (the Philippine Constabulary being the closest historical parallel), fitting a city that already wears its colonial architecture as corporate lobby decoration.

**Default stance: mostly honest, not systemically corrupt.** The rot in this setting isn't a department full of dirty cops — it's a genuinely functional police force that hits a hard, invisible ceiling the moment a case touches something [[The Compact]] cares about. That's a more useful default for a player-detective than blanket corruption: the badge means something for the majority of a case, which makes the moment it stops meaning something land harder.

## Funding & the Compact's Shadow

The Bureau's budget flows from SEZAC, and SEZAC's priorities flow from [[The Compact]] — so routine policing is underfunded almost by default. The Pinkerton parallel is the model here: a real detective agency whose loyalty always ultimately traced back to whoever was paying. When a case escalates to something Kaien Sōgō or Baekho Group actually cares about, resourcing and pressure arrive through channels that have nothing to do with the official chain of command — equipment with no requisition trail, a "consulting arrangement," a case quietly reassigned or buried. Most cops go a whole career without personally running into this. A detective whose "routine case" goes off the rails is exactly the kind of person who does.

## Jurisdiction Friction with Nusantara

The Constabulary handles crime; [[Nusantara Diaspora Resettlement and Workforce Solutions]] handles anything The Compact classifies as a corporate security threat — and those categories overlap constantly. A case can be pulled from CID and handed to Nusantara's contracted muscle the moment someone upstream decides it matters, with little the Constabulary can do about it. "Real cop vs. contracted gun for hire" is a standing, usable tension rather than a one-off plot device.

## Recruitment & the Squad Room

Unlike Nusantara, the Constabulary draws heavily from Saigon's actual resident population — effective policing needs language and community trust that an external contracted force doesn't have. Per [[Population and Demographics]], this makes CID itself a plausible cross-section of the zone's mix: Kinh, Hoa, archipelago-diaspora, and others sharing a squad room, with all the everyday friction that implies. A useful, low-effort source of recurring supporting cast without needing every character's background individually justified.

## The Player's Case

Consistent with the GDD's premise (§2, Player Premise & Story Hook): **Compact interference arrives later, not immediately.** The opening case should read and play as ordinary Constabulary work — routine, procedurally grounded, nothing that signals the player is being watched. The shift into Compact attention happens once the case has already started unraveling, so the tonal turn (official channels slowing down, resources appearing or disappearing without explanation, a colleague suddenly reluctant to help) becomes a mid-game beat the player feels rather than a premise they're told upfront.
